## Positioning

Six modules, one spine: **you don't learn tools, you install operating systems.**

Generic Claude Code content is saturated — Nick Saraev's 4-hour course sits at 2.2M
views, Dan Martell's 16-minute version at 2.0M. Competing there on coverage is a
losing game. What nobody owns is the **assembled system**: a Content OS, a Trading
OS, a Health OS — packaged harnesses that run someone's actual life. That is the
moat, and it is the one thing a general tutorial structurally cannot deliver.

| # | Module | Lessons | Job it does |
|---|--------|---------|-------------|
| 0 | From Cables to Claude (beginner bonus) | 8 | Perspective + on-ramp. Free, ungated. |
| 1 | Launch in 24 Hours | 10 | Proof of value. Every episode ships a live thing. |
| 2 | Claude Code L1 → L4 | 16 | The skill ladder, ending at "build your own harness". |
| 3 | AI Agents & Automations | 14 | Reliability + infrastructure. |
| 4 | AI Trading Floor | 16 | Highest willingness-to-pay. Strongest India whitespace. |
| 5 | AI Operating Systems | 16 | The moat. |

Format mix: 10–20 min theory shorts + 40–90 min full builds.
Delivery: recorded modules in a Skool classroom.
Audience floor: uses ChatGPT/Claude daily, has never opened a terminal.

## Go-to-market: sequenced, not simultaneous

**Situation.** 215 members, mostly inactive. Price gate rises at 400. Useful
material scattered across the classroom with no spine.

**Why not launch everything at once.** You have two assets in tension: 80 lessons of
content, and roughly *one* honest look from a dormant member before they tune out
permanently. A simultaneous launch spends the entire content arsenal to buy a single
shot at that attention. Sequenced drops convert the same content into repeated shots
— every wave is a fresh reason to email, post, and pull a lapsed member back.

The 400-member gate is the deadline mechanic, and it beats a fake countdown because
it is **true**. Use it in every touch.

### Wave 0 — Clean the house (before announcing anything)

- Audit every scattered classroom lesson: keep / re-record / archive.
- Re-file keepers into the six-module skeleton.
- Build the skeleton with all six modules **visible but locked**. Locked-and-visible
  is the whole trick — a returning member sees 80 lessons coming, not 12 sitting there.
- Write the pinned "what this community is now" post.
- Publish Module 0 free on YouTube during this window.

Do not skip this. Announcing a relaunch into a messy classroom burns the
re-engagement window you only get once.

### Wave 1 — Reactivation event: Launch in 24 Hours (weeks 1–3)

The only module where a member gets a live, shareable artifact within a day. Dormant
members don't return for curriculum; they return for a result.

Run it as a **live 24-hour build sprint**, not a passive drop. Fixed date, shared
kickoff, showcase thread at hour 24.

**Success metric: number of members who post a shipped thing** — not signups. That
is the number that restarts a dead community.

### Wave 2 — Claude Code L1→L4 (weeks 4–7)

The sprint creates the pain ("I shipped it but I don't know what I did"). L1→L4 is
the answer, which is why it lands second. Drop L1–L2 immediately, L3–L4 weekly.

### Wave 3 — Agents & Automations + AI Operating Systems (weeks 8–13)

Ship together: Agents is the theory, OS is the payoff. Content OS first (broadest
appeal), then Trading OS. This is where you cross 400 if Waves 1–2 landed.

### Wave 4 — AI Trading Floor (weeks 14–18)

Last, deliberately. Highest perceived value, so it justifies the **post-400 price**
rather than the pre-400 one. Also the heaviest production lift, and the strongest
standalone upsell if you ever split tiers.

## What the research actually said

**1. Gamma exposure is an empty room.** Best-performing GEX video on YouTube:
**119k views** (Rader Trader). Most sit under 60k. Meanwhile generic AI-agent
content does 3–4M. Institutional-grade perceived value, near-zero quality teaching,
and nobody has connected it to Claude at all. Single biggest whitespace in the plan.

**2. India + Claude + broker APIs is wide open.** Zerodha's own Kite MCP video: 404k.
marketfeed's Claude+Zerodha: 282k. Official channels validating demand into thin
supply. Every US-market trading tutorial is unusable for an Indian audience —
different brokers, taxes, market hours.

**3. Generic Claude Code tutorials are a bloodbath.** Saraev 2.2M, Martell 2.0M,
Swadia 1.5M, Tech With Tim 1.4M, AI Foundations 1.1M. Do not compete on coverage.
Frame L1→L4 as a **ladder with a named end state**, not a tutorial.

**4. "OS" framing is unclaimed.** Dan Martell gives away an "AI Company Operating
System" as a lead magnet; Nate Herk has a "free AI OS course". Nobody teaches
*building* one, connector by connector, with memory and scheduling. The term is in
the air and unowned.

---

## Beating a bigger channel: three patterns that work

1. **Narrower audience, total specificity.** They teach algo trading. You teach algo
   trading *on Kite, with Indian tax handling, in INR*.
2. **The failure they skipped.** Every big tutorial shows the happy path. Rate
   limits, silent cron failures, hallucinated figures — more valuable, and nobody
   films it because it is unglamorous.
3. **The assembled system.** They show one tool. You show six wired into something
   that runs unattended for 30 days.

## Research method

- **Tooling:** YouTube Data API v3 via Composio (`YOUTUBE_SEARCH_YOU_TUBE`,
  `YOUTUBE_GET_VIDEO_DETAILS_BATCH`). 32 topic queries ordered by `viewCount`,
  then 262 unique video IDs hydrated with `snippet`, `statistics`, `contentDetails`.
- **Filtering:** videos under 5 minutes excluded from benchmarking (Shorts skew).
- **Not used:** FireCrawl and yt-dlp — `api.firecrawl.dev` and `youtube.com` are both
  blocked by this environment's egress policy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT). The YouTube Data
  API returned better data anyway: exact view/like/comment counts rather than scraped
  approximations.
